# ADR-0002: PostgreSQL 18, managed by Render, with a standby and point-in-time recovery

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-19 (pooling and standby scope clarified the same day after the review panel)
- Decided by: Product owner, approving the recommended architecture package on 2026-10-19. Ravi confirms in M0.
- Confidence: High
- Change later: Hard

## Context
The data is relational: companies, staff, buildings, units, renters, maintenance requests, work orders, vendors and activity history. The owner said an hour of downtime is serious, which sets NFR-2 (99.9% monthly objective) and NFR-3 (lose at most 15 minutes of data; restore within 2 hours). Company isolation relies on row-level security (ADR-0003) and the job queue lives in the same database (ADR-0005). Budget: about $500 a month at launch.

## Options considered
1. **Render Postgres, same platform and region as the app** — high-availability standby with automatic failover; point-in-time recovery for 7 days on the Pro workspace; private network to the app / standby doubles the database cost; no Canadian region.
2. **A separately hosted managed PostgreSQL (for example Neon or Supabase)** — strong features / a second vendor on the critical path and network hops between providers.
3. **Self-managed PostgreSQL on a server** — cheapest compute / patching, failover and backups become the founders' job, which breaks the "no night-time work" criterion.
4. **A document database** — rejected: the data is relational (orders, statuses, permissions).

## Decision
Use Render Postgres, PostgreSQL 18, in the Ohio region next to the app: 1 CPU / 2 GB with a high-availability standby at launch, resized as load grows. The app connects over Render's private network as a role that does not own the tables (ADR-0003). Primary keys are UUID version 7, generated by PostgreSQL 18's `uuidv7()` as the column default, so IDs are not guessable in sequence and stay index-friendly. Render's 7-day point-in-time recovery is the first line of recovery; a nightly logical backup to separate storage (architecture.md › Operations) is the second.

## Why
- Meets NFR-2 and NFR-3: the standby takes over automatically after about 30 seconds of primary failure, and point-in-time recovery restores to any moment in the last 7 days.
- One provider for app and database keeps operations minimal and latency low.
- PostgreSQL provides row-level security, full-text search and JSON columns, so no second store is needed (NFR-6).

## Consequences
- Good: no database administration beyond sizing; restores are a dashboard action.
- Costs and tradeoffs: about $80 a month for primary and standby at launch, plus $0.30 per GB of storage on each. Replication is asynchronous, so a failover can lose a few seconds of writes. Small plans allow 100 connections: web instances use Django's built-in psycopg connection pool (at most 10 connections each), workers connect directly, and no external pooler is used, because the company context is transaction-local and Procrastinate needs LISTEN/NOTIFY. The standby protects against a failed database instance, not against an outage of the whole Render region (risks.md R-8). There is no Canadian region (risks.md R-7).
- Follow-up work: prove the owner, app and metrics roles on Render in the M0 spike and build them in M1; run a restore drill before the pilot and every quarter.

## Evidence
- [Render pricing](https://render.com/pricing.md) (checked 2026-10-19) — 1 CPU / 2 GB Postgres $40 a month; storage $0.30/GB; point-in-time recovery 7 days on Pro and above, 3 days on Hobby.
- [Render high availability](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-high-availability) (checked 2026-10-19) — needs at least 1 CPU; standby billed like the primary; failover after 30 seconds of unavailability; "no more than a few seconds of changes are lost" in almost all cases.
- [Render PostgreSQL versions](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-upgrading) (checked 2026-10-19) — versions 13–18 fully supported.
- [Django 6.1 release notes](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/6.1/releases/6.1/) (checked 2026-10-19) — PostgreSQL 15 and later.

## Revisit when
- Database CPU stays above 70% or storage passes 200 GB (resize first).
- Reports (FR-20) slow down staff pages (add a read replica).
- A customer requires Canadian data residency (risks.md R-7).
- Render database incidents affect the product more than twice in a quarter.
